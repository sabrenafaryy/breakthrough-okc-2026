// The Problem

Why The Margin Keeps Shrinking

You're shopping where everyone shops

Finished, listed, and visible to every investor with the same alerts you have. Competing on price is the only lever left when you're all looking at the same inventory.

The tired building looks like a problem

It is a problem — until you can read which street turns next and what public money is already committed to turning it. Then it's the only cheap thing left.

You've never priced the hold

Some of the buildings you pass on are worth keeping rather than selling — and the money that rewards holding is a different set of doors than the hard money you already know.

// The Evidence

What The Map Actually Says To A Flipper

Straight answer: most public housing money in this state is development and affordable-housing finance, and a short-hold flip does not qualify for it. What the day gives you is the map of which doors are real for you — and which one is worth changing your exit for.

6 people

That's how many claimants statewide were approved for the historic rehab credit in 2023 — a credit worth a combined 40% of qualified rehab costs when Oklahoma's 20% is stacked with the federal 20%. Read the catch before you get excited: it requires an income-producing use and carries a five-year federal recapture, so a short-hold flip forfeits it. This is the number that makes people run the rehab-and-hold version of a deal for the first time.

Source: Oklahoma Incentive Evaluation Commission, Oct 2024 (state 20% + federal 26 U.S.C. §47 20%) · accessed Aug 27, 2026Source: 26 U.S.C. §50(a) — five-year recapture on the rehabilitation credit · accessed Aug 27, 2026
$39.7M

Of the NE Renaissance TIF districts' $50M project budget, $39.7M is still unallocated per the city's FY25 report — budget capacity against future increment, not cash at closing. You are unlikely to tap it on a flip, but knowing where a district's boundary sits is part of reading which street turns next.

Source: City of OKC FY25 TIF Annual Report, as of June 30, 2025 · accessed Aug 27, 2026
$316,450

The median home listing price in the OKC metro in July 2026. A low basis is why the rehab math still works in this market — and why the margin is worth defending with better sourcing rather than a higher bid.

Source: Realtor.com listing data, July 2026 · accessed Aug 27, 2026
